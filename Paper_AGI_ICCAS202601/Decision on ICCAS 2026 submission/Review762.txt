Reviewer 1 of ICCAS 2026 submission 346

Comments to the author
======================

1. The LLM-based search procedure is not sufficiently
reproducible.

The LLM-based search is one of the main contributions, but
the paper provides only a high-level description. The
manuscript states that OpenAI GPT-5.5 with medium reasoning
effort is used through Codex with tools, and that the model
reads previous trial logs and proposes the next four MPC
weights. However, the exact decision procedure is not
reproducible.

2. The fairness of the comparison between LLM-based search
and BO should be examined more carefully.

All methods are given the same number of simulator
evaluations, which is reasonable when simulation cost
dominates. However, LLM-based search and BO have
substantially different candidate-selection overheads.

3. The experimental evaluation is limited to a single
slalom scenario.

The proposed conclusions are based on one CarMaker nominal
slalom scenario with the target speed increased by 5% to 61
km/h. This is insufficient to establish general utility for
autonomous-vehicle MPC calibration.

4. The reported number of repetitions is too small for
strong statistical conclusions.

Each method is repeated only five times. This is a small
sample for stochastic search algorithms, especially when
the paper emphasizes differences in success probability and
early feasibility discovery.

5. The definition and detection of cone contacts and road
departure need more detail.

Cone contacts are central to the feasibility criterion, yet
the paper does not fully explain how they are detected and
counted. The statement that entry contacts before the
evaluation gate are ignored also requires justification.

6. The sparse-feasible-region claim should be interpreted
more carefully.

The paper reports that only 7 of 4096 Sobol samples are
hit-free and concludes that feasible regions are sparse.
This is suggestive, but the statement is stronger than the
evidence supports.

7. Generalization of the tuned MPC weights is not
evaluated.

The paper searches for weights that perform well on the
same scenario used for evaluation. However, practical
calibration requires robustness across operating
conditions.

8. The MPC implementation lacks key reproducibility
details.

The paper provides sample time and horizons but omits
several important controller parameters.

9. The novelty relative to prior LLM-assisted Bayesian
optimization work should be sharpened.

The related-work section cites LLM-assisted BO and
closed-loop experimentation studies, but the manuscript
should articulate more clearly what is methodologically
new. At present, the main novelty appears to be applying an
LLM as a sequential candidate proposer for MPC weight
tuning in CarMaker.
